BP-002 · Thermodynamics

Four-stroke engine

125 cc air-cooled DOHC single in section through the cylinder and valve axes

Turning heat into rotation through a four-phase cycle

An Otto cycle in a finned cylinder. The cams, turning at half crank speed, open each valve by the lift their profile gives; a computed cycle at 6000 rpm (CR 9.2, 58 bar peak) sets the gas, which changes from fresh charge to a flame growing from the plug to burned gas. The P–V diagram traces the loop as the crank turns, with the pumping loop visible on its log scale.

BP-003 · Control / steam

Porter governor

Porter flyball governor with bevel drive, bell crank and double-beat steam throttle valve, in axial section

Negative-feedback proportional control: the speed sets the opening, so more load is carried at a lower speed (droop)

Two iron balls on equal arms lift a weighted sleeve as the spindle speeds up, and the sleeve shuts a balanced double-beat valve through a bell crank. When the load steps up the engine slows, the balls drop, the valve opens, and the engine settles at a new, slightly lower speed. That offset is the droop of a proportional controller, plotted against the governor's own characteristic.

BP-004 · Horology

Lever escapement

Swiss lever watch escapement in plan, with a section through the balance staff and its jewel bearings

Turning stored energy into equal ticks: a clock-driven scheduler, a rate-limited event loop

A balance and hairspring swing at 4 Hz whatever the mainspring does; the escapement only lets the train move when the balance says so. Each beat the fork unlocks a stone, the club tooth pushes the stone and the fork pushes the impulse jewel, then the tooth drops onto the other stone and locks, so the escape wheel advances one tooth every two beats. The diagram marks every impulse on the balance's swing.

  • Horology teaching: lock, unlock, impulse, drop, draw and lift angle
  • Product pages and documentation for mechanical watches
  • Explaining a clock-driven scheduler or rate limiter: an independent oscillator releases stored work in fixed quanta

BP-005 · Hydraulics

Bottle jack

2-tonne hydraulic bottle jack in axial section

Force multiplication by area ratio (Pascal's principle)

Pascal's principle in a cast base. Each 25 mm stroke of the Ø12 plunger pushes 2.83 cm³ of oil past the delivery ball and lifts the Ø32 ram 3.52 mm, so 138 N on the handle holds 1.5 t. Section A-A puts the two bores side by side, and cracking the release screw lets the load drive the oil back to the reservoir.

BP-006 · Hydraulics / software concepts

Cistern token bucket

Close-coupled WC cistern with a diaphragm float valve and a pull-knob drop valve, in section

Token bucket rate limiting (API rate limits)

The water is the tokens. A fixed Ø3.5 jet at mains pressure refills at 0.146 L/s whatever the level, which is a constant token rate, and the float shuts it at capacity. Each pull of the knob takes one 1.5 L dose; below one token the dose float rests on its stop and the pull is refused, and the overflow tube is the hard cap if the float is held down.

BP-008 · Mechanical security

Pin tumbler cylinder

Five-pin euro-profile half cylinder (DIN 18252, 30/10) and its key in longitudinal section

Authentication by matching a secret: every factor must match (AND)

Each key pin is cut for one depth of the key, so the right key lifts every crown exactly to the shear line between plug and body. The key is cut in 0.5 mm steps; a key one cut wrong leaves pin 3 a millimetre low, its driver straddles the line and the plug will not turn. Only when all five gaps sit on the line does the plug, and its cam, turn.

  • Security-concept explainers: shared secrets, multi-factor checks as a logical AND
  • Locksmith and security-hardware training: bitting codes, depth steps, shear line

BP-009 · Electrical protection

Circuit breaker

B16 miniature circuit breaker (EN 60898-1) cut on its centre plane

The circuit breaker pattern: closed, open, half-open

A bimetal heated by its own I²R bends until it knocks the latch out, so an overload trips after seconds along an inverse-time curve, while above 4 × In the coil's plunger strikes at once. After a trip the latch only holds a reset once the strip has cooled: open, then half-open, then closed again, or straight back to open if the fault is still there.

BP-010 · Thermal control

Wax thermostat

Automotive wax-pellet coolant thermostat (opens at 88 °C) in section, in its housing between engine and radiator

A setpoint controller with no electronics: negative feedback through melting wax

Paraffin in a brass cup gains 12 % in volume as it melts, squeezes a rubber sleeve and pushes the pin against the bridge, so the cup moves down: the valve opens to the radiator and the bypass disc shuts. The engine's heat balance closes the loop, and the coolant settles a little above 88 °C, higher under load. Wax freezes 2 K below where it melts, so the lift–temperature diagram traces a hysteresis loop.

  • Control engineering teaching: proportional control, droop, hysteresis and feedback without electronics

Relief valve

DN25 spring-loaded safety relief valve in axial section

A spring-set threshold with hysteresis: pop open, blow down, reseat

The spring's preload over the seat area sets the opening pressure. Once the disc cracks open, gas fills the huddling chamber and acts on a larger area, so the valve pops to high lift and stays open until the pressure has fallen about 10 %, then snaps shut. A compressor refills the receiver and the cycle repeats, tracing the hysteresis loop in Diagram 1.

BP-012 · Automotive drivetrain

Open differential

Rear-axle open bevel-gear differential in plan section, with the car in a turn

Splitting one input between two outputs that may run at different speeds: load balancing

The crown wheel turns the carrier, and the carrier always turns at the mean of the two wheels. The spider gears stay still on their pin when the car runs straight and spin only when the wheels differ, in a turn or with one wheel on ice. Because a spider pushes equally on both side gears, the torque splits equally, so the wheel on ice sets what both wheels get.

  • Automotive engineering and mechanics courses: differential kinematics, ωC = (ωL + ωR)/2
  • Product and service documentation for axles and final drives

BP-016 · Hydraulics

External gear pump

Group 2 external gear pump, 8 cm³/rev, cut across and along its shafts

Positive displacement: a conveyor of fixed-size batches

Two involute gears carry oil round the outside of the bore in sealed tooth spaces, 24 batches of 0.33 cm³ per revolution, and the mesh squeezes them out at the outlet. Flow is displacement × speed less a laminar leak that grows with pressure, so the volumetric efficiency falls as pressure rises, and falls further at low speed. Each space's pressure builds over the last two pitches before the outlet.

  • Fluid power courses: displacement, volumetric efficiency, leakage and torque
  • Product pages and data sheets for hydraulic gear pumps

BP-017 · Fluid mechanics

Carburettor

Fixed-jet float-bowl carburettor, 26 mm bore, in axial section

Bernoulli's principle: one flow metering another

Air speeding through the venturi throat loses ½ρv² of pressure, and because the float bowl is vented that depression lifts petrol through the main jet, so fuel follows air in proportion. Below the threshold the pilot circuit takes over through the idle and progression ports; the float drops as the jets draw, and its needle opens to refill the bowl.

  • Engine and motorcycle courses: venturi depression, jetting, idle and progression circuits
  • Workshop and product documentation for small-engine carburettors

BP-018 · Thermodynamics

Pressure cooker

6 L stovetop pressure cooker (EN 12778) in axial section

Raising the boiling point with pressure; a safety interlock that cannot open while the resource is in use

Water boils where its vapour pressure meets the pressure above it, so a 62 g weight on a Ø3 bore holds 0.86 bar and the water boils at 118 °C instead of 100. The lock pin rises at 3 kPa, seals its own vent and blocks the lid's slide, so the lid cannot be turned while the pot is pressurised. The operating point climbs the saturation curve, sits on the weight while the steam vents, and slides back down when the hob goes off.

  • Thermodynamics teaching: vapour pressure, the Antoine equation, latent heat and boiling point against pressure
  • Product documentation and safety training for pressure cookers and autoclaves
  • Explaining a lock that guards a resource: the lid stays locked for as long as the pressure (the work in progress) holds

BP-019 · Hydraulics and electromagnetics

Solenoid valve

2/2-way normally closed pilot-operated solenoid valve, G1/2, in axial section

A small signal controlling a big flow: servo amplification through a pilot

A 10 W coil lifts an 11 mm plunger only 3.2 mm, and that opens a 1.6 mm pilot. The pilot drains the chamber above the diaphragm faster than the 0.8 mm bleed refills it, so water pressure lifts the diaphragm after the plunger and 35 L/min flows. Switched off, the bleed lets the diaphragm down slowly enough (0.72 s) that the surge in the pipe stays below 1 bar.

  • Industrial automation and fluid-power training: pilot operation, MOPD, minimum differential pressure, water hammer
  • Product documentation for solenoid and diaphragm valves
  • Explaining amplification, or a feature flag as a small pilot signal that switches a large flow

BP-020 · Thermodynamics

Stirling engine

Beta-type Stirling demonstrator with a rhombic drive, in axial section

Heat flowing from hot to cold does work: the Stirling cycle

A burner heats the cap, the displacer shuttles one sealed charge of air between the hot cap and the finned cooler through a 1 mm gap regenerator, and the power piston takes work from the pressure swing. A Schmidt model drives everything: the gas is shaded by the temperature of the space it is in, the P–V loop traces live, and turning the burner down shrinks the loop and slows the flywheel towards its stall.

  • Thermodynamics teaching: the Stirling cycle, Schmidt analysis, Carnot and the role of the regenerator
  • Explaining heat engines and why a temperature difference is needed to do work
  • Product or museum pages for demonstrator engines and rhombic drives

BP-021 · Hydraulics

Hydraulic disc brake

Bicycle hydraulic disc brake: lever master cylinder and two-piston post-mount caliper in section

Hydraulic force transmission; a master–replica pair where one piston writes and the other follows

Pascal's principle between two pistons. The lever's primary cup first has to pass the timing port before any pressure can build, then the oil pushes the caliper pistons across the pad gap, and only once the pads touch the rotor does the clamp force climb steeply. On release the square seals spring back and pull the pistons off the rotor by exactly the rollback they took.

  • Cycling mechanics training: bleeding, free stroke, bite point and pad clearance
